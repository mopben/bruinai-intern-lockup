Video to actions

The pipeline in vision/ takes a filmed video of café workers, finds and follows each person, puts them on the floor in metres and cuts their time into actions such as work, fetch and carry. A vision-language model can then name the action. Below, each stage runs on the best data we have, and says how well it did.

  1. 1Videofixed camera, filmed after the run
  2. 2TracksRF-DETR finds people, ByteTrack follows them
  3. 3Positionsfloor point in metres, 10 times a second
  4. 4Actionswork, fetch, carry, walk, idle
  5. 5Labelsoptional words from a model; experimental

Two of us make a drink + chips combo at a kitchen island: the drink worker pours juice at the door end and walks each drink over to the chips worker, who puts chips on plates at the dining end. The video is a handheld phone, held up about 2 m, steadied afterwards. The boxes are the tracker's, drawn live; we gave the two workers their names by hand for this clip, and the people at the table and outside are left out. There was no tablet, so there are no taps.

Floor positions are not shown: the camera was only 20 to 30 cm above the workers' heads, so a few centimetres of head height moves a point on the floor by metres, and the worker behind the island never shows his feet.

    Measured against the dataset's hand-drawn boxes over all 795 frames. The tracker used 28 track ids for 19 people: a person who is hidden for a while often comes back with a new number. The floor error mostly measures how our box edges and the hand-drawn ones disagree: the ground truth comes from those boxes, and in this scene one pixel of box edge is worth about 7 cm on the ground. It does not test a café camera indoors.

    02

    Positions become actions

    SYNTHETIC RUN: NO VIDEO

    This is counter-a, the bundled computer-generated run: three workers making ten lattes in 88.6 s. Its positions were made by a simulation, not by a camera. The pipeline's actions step turns them into segments from where each worker stands and where they go, and we compare those with the "done" taps on the tablet.

      The step found 46 action segments. 23 of 30 "done" taps fall inside the tapping worker's work segment at that station, and 7 more come within 2 s after one ends. Two limits are built into the method: Ana at the till never moves, so she is work for 100% of the run, as positions cannot tell waiting from working; and a fetch shorter than 0.5 m shows as walk.

      03

      Naming the action

      REAL VIDEO: BREAKFAST ACTIONS

      Positions say where someone works, not what they do. The optional label step shows a model a few frames from each 2 s window and asks it to pick from a short list. We tested two models on 26 home-kitchen clips of people making coffee, tea and milk drinks, tuned on half the people and scored on the other half (P16, P23, P50).         Each model was offered the 4 to 6 labels of the clip's drink and saw the whole frame. Labels are smoothed over neighbouring windows (Gemini 5, SigLIP 3, each chosen on the tuning half). An action counts as found when a predicted segment with the same label overlaps it by at least half. Gemini cost 633 requests, 3.36 million tokens and 30 minutes (4 requests at a time) to label all 26 clips, 20.9 minutes of video. SigLIP is about as good as always guessing the most common action. Gemini is much better here, but still wrong on about a third of frames, and neither was measured on café footage.

        04

        A real café clip

        REAL VIDEO: QUALITATIVE ONLY

        A 9.5 s stock clip of two people behind a bar. The tracker found both and kept their numbers. We then asked both models what each person was doing. There is no scan or calibration of this bar, so the action segments the labels attach to are stand-ins: one 9.4 s idle segment per person, made by hand.

        0.0 s
        PersonWhat they do, by eyeGemini nowSigLIP now

        Gemini gets the man right after 4 s ("talking") and misses the woman: she is drying glasses with a cloth, which it calls "talking". SigLIP says "waiting" for both throughout. Two people for 9 s is an anecdote, not a measurement. The clip also pans slightly, which a real setup must not. No real café run with a scan and a calibration exists yet, so the full chain from video to positions and actions has only run on synthetic tracks.

        What is not verified

        • Any real café run end to end: video, scan and calibration of one kitchen.
        • A high camera tilted down indoors.
        • Counters hiding the lower body, with the floor point taken from the top of the head.
        • Workers sharing a station (told apart only by the order they are listed in).
        • Customers leaning over the counter into the working area.
        • Phone video with a variable frame rate (frames are assumed evenly spaced).
        • Labels on café footage, with either model.
